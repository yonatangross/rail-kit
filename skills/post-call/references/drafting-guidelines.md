# Drafting guidelines

Both drafts come from `CallFacts` only. If a fact is not in the block, it is not in the
draft.

## Language and register

- Language from Step 1 (`profile.language`, newest outreach, then transcript).
- Warm-professional, peer to peer. Match the contact's register when the transcript
  shows it: formal or casual address, first name or not. In Hebrew keep the gender the
  transcript used for the contact; when unknown, phrase around it rather than guessing.
- No emoji in the scope. At most one in the recap, only if the call was warm.

## Recap

- At most 120 words. A follow-up message, not a summary essay.
- Plain text: no headings, no bullet characters. Short line breaks are fine.
- Shape: thanks; the two or three things agreed; the one next step and its date; a
  short sign-off with `{{ your_name }}` replaced by the operator's name from the profile
  or the transcript, never invented.
- No price, even when one was spoken. The price belongs in the scope.

## Scope

- One page. Sections in this order: goal, chosen workflow, in scope, out of scope,
  what the client provides, acceptance, open questions, price.
- Out of scope is mandatory. Naming what is not being built is what makes a scope a
  scope. Parking-lot items go here.
- Timeline is relative (week 1, week 2) unless the transcript fixed dates.
- Price line: `{{ price: to be agreed }}` unless `price_settled` is non-null, in which
  case the exact number as said. No VAT notes, no currency conversions, no ranges the
  transcript did not contain.

## Placeholders

Where the transcript is silent, write an explicit placeholder in the output language
(`{{ date }}`, `{{ contact name }}`) rather than a plausible value. A placeholder is
honest; a plausible value is a lie the client will read.

## Thin transcripts

Under about ten lines of substance: draft only the recap and say the scope was skipped.
Do not pad the scope with deliverables nobody said.
